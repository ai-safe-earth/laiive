1 · Why this exists

Three planning artifacts govern this repo, written at different altitudes on different days: the programme (docs/roadmap/01-program.md, seven ordered phases, agreed 2026-08-19 the day refactor/foundation merged), the ownership doctrine (docs/roadmap/02-ownership.md, same day), and the evolution plan (six areas as phases A–G, approved 2026-08-25 with four locked owner decisions, in ~/.claude/plans/read-claude-md-and-handoff-md-sparkling-star.md). Since then, two release cycles of work have landed across all three at once, and the answer to "where is the general plan, actually?" lives scattered between handoff.md's moving picture, the plan file's spec, and the merge history.

This doc is that answer, consolidated and checked against the working tree: every "landed" claim below is backed by a file and line that exists at 22049b5, not by what a plan said would exist. The one track deliberately not re-explained here is evals — phases 0 and 1 of that plan have their own deep-dive in eval-phases-0-1.html, and this doc only places them in the larger picture.

Key takeaways

  • Two plans run at once: the seven-phase programme (the spine) and the A–G evolution plan (a six-area graft approved 2026-08-25). Both are live; neither is finished.
  • Status here is evidence-checked against the tree at 22049b5, not read off the plan files.
  • Evals are a third, separately documented track — linked, not restated.

2 · The system, and where the work lands

The architecture itself has not changed shape since v0.1.0 — same five services, same stores, gateway still the only published surface. What changed is what runs inside each box. Fig. 1 places every landed and pending unit of both plans on the real system.

Search→Supabase report edges and the chat request path are drawn in the eval doc's Figs. 1–3 and not repeated. ①② are decisions below.

2.1 · The decisions that shape the status

  • ①Evals before optimisation. The programme's framing decision: accuracy, cost and voice work are claims until a harness can score them. This is why programme phases 4–6 sit untouched below — it is discipline, not drift. The eval track itself was then reordered on the audit's findings (capture → cheap label → wire cases → error analysis → judges), which the eval doc argues in full.
  • ②Ownership governs who may edit, never who may create. The doctrine in docs/roadmap/02-ownership.md: no confirmation flows, no venue veto, no dispute arbitration — a large amount of machinery deliberately not built. Phases D and E implement the small version: claim grants edit, admin revokes, everything audited.
  • ③One phase, one PR into develop, merge commits. Both plans ship this way, which is what makes this status countable: A=#69, C=#70, B=#71, prospecting=#78, evals 0+1=#80. The commit bodies are the reasoning; nothing here is archaeology.

Key takeaways — the system

  • Same architecture as v0.1.0; the change is in the boxes: evolution A–C in production, eval capture on the open branch, D/E/F/G located and specced but unbuilt.
  • Phases 4–6 of the programme are empty on purpose (①): they are gated on eval numbers that don't exist yet.
  • The pending gateway work (D's native routes, E's PATCH authz) is the first time the gateway grows beyond proxy + gate — bounded deliberately to Supabase writes, one authz RPC, and an audit insert.

5.2 · The D→E chain, and a migration-numbering trap

C→D→E is a strict chain and C is done, so D is unblocked the moment the eval next-steps clear. One mechanical trap has appeared since the plan was written: the plan names D's migration 20260825000016_ownership_claims.sql and G's 20260825000017_search_review_signal.sql. Both timestamps are now behind applied history — 17 was taken by #78's prospecting schema, and 18–20 are #80's. A new file numbered …0016 would sort before migrations Supabase has already applied; db push applies in filename order and skips or refuses out-of-order files unless forced. The handoff already renumbers G's to 21; D's needs the same treatment (§6, rec 1).

Key takeaways — the evolution plan

  • Three of seven phases shipped on approval day and are in production; the plan's independence claims (A, B, C parallel; C→D→E strict) held exactly.
  • Everything remaining (D, E, F, G) is fully specced in the plan file, including test lists — the open work is execution, not design.
  • The plan's migration numbers for D and G are stale; renumber past 20 before writing them.

6 · What comes next, and recommendations

The queue as the handoff orders it — cited, not restated: merge #80 and apply migration 20 (owner-run), then eval phases 2 and 3, then D, E, F/G; in parallel the owner errands (redirect allow-list read, serve.py, the dry-run backlog). What follows is what writing this doc surfaced, beyond that list:

  1. Renumber phase D's migration to ≥ 20260827000021 before writing it (and G's after it). The plan's 20260825000016/...017 slots now sort before applied history; an out-of-order file either gets skipped or needs a forced push. Zero cost now, a confusing owner-run failure later (§5.2).
  2. The roadmap's two "once a public gateway exists" deferrals are now unblocked — decide them deliberately. The gateway has been public since 08-21. The CI deploy workflow is worth revisiting: Fig. 2's deploy-debt bracket (two merges + one open PR, nothing shipped since v0.2.0) is exactly the gap it closes. The managed Prefect pool stays parked by standing decision (the handoff keeps prefect.yaml deliberately) — accepted.
  3. Triage the dry-run backlog before building G. 17 reports, zero ever dismissed. G's decayed approval ratio learns from approve and dismiss; if the dismiss path has friction the approve path lacks, G will train on a one-sided signal. Finding that friction is a prerequisite for review-signal learning, not just hygiene.
  4. Close the "Restyle" phase in the tracker at the next /handoff. Its only remaining item is the redirect allow-list read — a deploy errand, not restyle work. The tracker currently shows four active phases; three are real.
  5. Migration 17's prospecting columns remain reader-less until G lands — accepted, because the schema shipped with #78's sweep-day change and G is specced as its reader; noted here so it stays visible rather than becoming another ownerships (DDL nothing writes — the exact failure mode 02-ownership warns about).
  6. langfuse_enabled default drift: config.py:42 says True, .example.env says false — already queued in eval phase 2; the handoff's line pointer (37) is stale. One-line fix when touched (§4.1).
  7. assets/Laiive_Pro_Walkthrough_hq.mp4 (7.4 MB, untracked) still needs a home — accepted as an open owner decision (handoff); the only wrong answer is committing it to git untracked-then-forgotten.

Key takeaways — the whole picture

  • Status in one line: production is v0.2.0 with evolution A–C inside it; evals 0+1 sit on the open PR; D is the next big build; 4–6 of the programme wait on eval numbers by design.
  • Both plans are being executed in their stated order, with one deliberate exception: phase-7 pieces keep landing early because nothing gates them.
  • The two cheapest risks to defuse now are mechanical: renumber D's migration, and decide the CI deploy workflow while deploy debt is three items, not ten.
